
 not engaging worthwhile things ➡️🥯doing it when its hard🥯⬅️ working miserably

The "give up" if it makes you miserable is great advice for someone who doesn't know when to quit. In this case, I need to know that some things are worth doing even if I feel miserable and hopeless. In these past few months, I have been feeling intermittently miserable, which puts a pause on my applying (which doesn't actually stop the misery). Recently, I haven't been applying, feeling miserable, with the external expectations of the unemployed. I didn't want to do anything and felt worse doubly by not doing anything. There was nothing I wanted to do. It led to a day and night of crying because I was miserable in absence of activity. "Give up" wasn't even an option. 
After I was encouraged to try again, I felt buoyed by enough hope to chart a course again and believe it would be good to do. Today, I made it out to Woodstock today to meet Marissa. I had thoughts, "*Even this is pointless. Not even a job interview. I drove this far out? What will my family say... I don't even want to be here*". But I had God abiding in me quietly and had a present to live, so I resigned to doing it, even feeling hopeless. The meeting was helpful in content and in lifting my spirits once again. Regardless, it doesn't matter how I feel entering into it; how useful something actually is or could be is independent than what thoughts I have about it.

And that's what I want to remember: **how useful something is or could be is independent than what perceptions I have about it.** So I ought to try, even if my emotional state or assumptions give me reasons to not engage at all.
