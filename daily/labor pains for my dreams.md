Dad and I got into a loud and honest exchange over our personal philosophies and how that affects my job search. Lately I have been discouraged by the 6th month anniversary of my unemployment, which leaks in my dragging feet, constant tears, and sad demeanor. So on the car ride after Mass, he addresses the elephant in the room. And his support comes in the form of evaluation, which I already accepted. What hurts was the "do more!". Nonstop crying. He didn't like my attitude that "I don't like the work", even if I do what's needful. "It's not about strength, it's about persistence!" when all I do is what I can and it's not enough. I cry and cry and cry because it's so painful. It's true that I did not prepare enough, and I should do more for next time. It is also true that I am doing so much and it sounds like he's telling me by "put your 100% into it!" that I'm not doing it right, that I am not doing enough. I am tired and discouraged and sad. And I am going to keep doing the things ("apply anywhere! Everywhere!"), and I AM! It's so tedious and I hate it. "The right answer is 'yes! I will do better and be overprepared next time!'"

He encourages medical school because that's "the top", job security, power, respect, freedom, the whole gambit. That is enough for Chris. Joyce and Joey don't even need a whole gambit to be happy. But I don't even want the gambit, and chose this foggy path even knowing that I might not have those things. Dad means well and I am doing everything I can BECAUSE he and Mom give me everything I need to go wherever I want. He suggests medical school because that, at the very least, can get me a job. Mom understands completely what I want to do though, and now has a little more insight into my troubles. I could have been completely convinced today that medical school was the best way out. I may not have anyone in the professional world listening to me, but anyone listens to a doctor. I may not know how exactly to "make it" in the public health field, but I know how to be an excellent premedical student. In that way, medical school was the path of least resistance. Wouldn't you do the same? If someone told you that going to medical school would make all the insecurities of what you don't know what to do, all the pain go away? I was a premed for 2 hours today before Mom questioned what I wanted to do, and encouraged me that getting one person in the WHO or IDPH to listen to me was a hell of a lot easier than 5 years of medical training. So we go forward applying to the WHO and IDPH. 

If Dad asks to help me apply again, I will let him. I think what I need from him, and from myself, most of all, is patience.
