
I was flying out from Houston to Durango on a weekday, and, after much deliberation, my steadfast bestie agreed to drive me to the airport in the morning before he would head back downtown for work. Already over the weekend, we had discovered together (very, very quickly for him) that my pitchy, lyric-guessing singing rage-baited him to the point of banning English songs. He admitted to being more annoyed than usual since he was on a self-imposed phone ban, but I found his very honest hating hilarious anyway, even as he muted the music. Then, we ran into traffic. He wasn't in the mood to talking and joking either, and admitted out loud that "he should have let me take the Uber".

If any other friend had said this driving me to the airport, I would have been hurt. Instead, from him, I felt so loved by his honesty.

> I think I have no other friend who is more boundary pushing and is comfortable with expressing his negative emotions. Anyone else would have kept that irritation to themself! 
As well, I didn't know he was accommodating me until the end of the trip; he gave up junky food but ate it because I was around. He was also in his sleep by 8, wake at 5 to hit the gym. Not when I was there; we regularly woke around 7am and hung out until the end of day. We didn't even hit the gym. He dissuaded me from running after I threw up, but let me run the 8th mile because I felt up to it. 
Source: [[HTX from the Houston tour]]

It rings true that *emotions are neutral*, and the action emotions "motion" us to is what has moral character. I love that he is comfortable to express his emotions as they're evoked and I already know that he cares for me -- it is not a slight or an attack in the least. 

> I wonder what it takes to be *honest*.
