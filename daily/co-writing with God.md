
As Fr. Mike Schmitz listed in his *Autobiography* homily series, there are 4 components of a strong start for every protagonist: irreversibility, identity at stake, a risk (or uncertain future), and agency. He identified Frodo, Adam and Eve, Jesus, and the Prodigal Son as different examples who had "strong starts". 

See these major *strong starts* in my own story:
1. my choice to do [[the work of God]], a progression of my strong start to begin writing with God in whatever I do
2. my choice to pursue public health for my career ([[blueprints for a castle]])
3. my choice to enter medical school ([[medical school arc]]) as a stepping stone for my public health career

These are the starts that can be encapsulated with an *irreversible* tipping point, my *identity* at stake, a risk *where* the future is uncertain, and an exercise of *agency*. Even my slow, strong starts to commit to writing ([[the first brick]]) and to running a marathon have deeply shaped my human experience. 
- By **writing**, I strengthen my ability to reflect. If to reflect, then to be aware and make decisions. And if to be aware and make decisions, then to use my agency and move with purpose. By moving on purpose, I live my co-authorship with God for my story. Every time I have brought up the a'Kempis quote "man proposes, God disposes", it is as if I am proposing to God, as a co-author, my plan for the future, and giving it to Him to write it into the story or not. 
- In **running**, and especially this Lenten season, I learn how my body and soul influence another. I understand the links between my corporal and spiritual realities more closely than ever, and am able to influence them to my benefit. Additionally, this also applies to understanding my mental and psychosocial realities as I understand them through the HGG guide and in conversation.

*For the future?* I intend to plan a start for reconnecting with my mandarin duck ([[a pair of mandarin ducks]]). So far, my strong starts have concerned "*what?*", but this is the second time since choosing the Lord that, [[using my agency]], I would choose "*who?*". Once I make the time to reach out, again and again and again, then it becomes irreversible. God willing, we become no longer strangers, but friends, with the risk of further love and grief.
