> As long as my breath is in me, and the spirit of God is in my nostrils; my lips will not speak falsehood, and my tongue will not utter deceit. Far be it from me to say that you are right; till I die I will not put away my **integrity** from me.
> Job upholding honesty and his claim to righteousness in response to his friends[^1]

This is a reflection on the various origins of my personal motivation ([[motivation MOC]]). I attempt to reckon with my actions and elucidate my values, so that I may think, speak, and act in greater accordance with my values. To become a person of character is to cultivate virtue. To have integrity, I must value virtue and act according to my claimed values.

I value *truth*, but, at times, not as much as I value *comfort*. 
I value *mercy*, but, at times, not as much as I value *safety*.
I value *growth*, but value for my *ego*, my *identity comfort/safety*, keeps me positioned to merely "should" take more opportunities to grow.

##### Example: Getting out of bed. 
One of my 2026 goals is figuring out [[how to get out of bed]]. It is a **fight**! Because I am not motivated to do so most mornings; it is always "I *should* get out of bed", but what I really value in that moment is my own comfort.
	How can I *reframe* "getting out of bed" to make it easy?
1. Find a reason to care.
- Do it for God, for family, for curiosity, for potential surprises, for delight, because the best time to begin the day is here! 
	- These I *care about* and want to be more mindful of, to consistently **value them more than** my own comfort.
1. Accept it as an obligation, a duty, to get out of bed.
	This is less fun (and less recommendable) to attempt reframing as a motivator, if I have a choice; but I would likely identify obligations that take precedence over comfort, and then bear the cost of sacrificing my comfort to fulfill morning obligations.

***ACTION***: to remind myself of my values, let it be that my first words of the day be a Psalm ("Lord, open my lips; and my mouth will proclaim your praise"[^2]), a *fiat*, or a heartfelt "good morning!" -- because that is the reality of the day, even on the days where you are most afflicted with inertia, fatigue, and desolation.
