The natural step after learning to [[walk with God]] is walking with others. When we walk with God, we receive His love ([[receiving others like God]]) and learn how to love in turn. To walk with, to abide, to dwell with is to share one's very presence. Shall we not also love our neighbors by walking with them?

This charism distinguishes both Ibram, "exalted father", and Pope Francis, the exalted papa. What does walking with others look like? It looks like standing up for the shy, Black girl overlooked and remembering the poor, holding esteem and love for the hometown prostitutes. 

Connor asked that, that he doesn't know what to do when it comes to big, systemic problems. The answer is just that, showing up for others and dismantling oppression in all its forms. Oppression is pervasive and comes in words, ideas, and policies. Racism, poverty, the degradation of the earth; these are pressing issues. It is something to be active against. Passivity only serves to maintain oppressive structures in place. Ibram marks no safe "middle ground" between anti-oppression and oppression. We are either [[in solidarity]] with others, or we remain in opposition to their suffering.
