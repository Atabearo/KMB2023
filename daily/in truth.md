I fear I rage-baited my friend out of an interesting discussion and into frustration.

This [[steadfast friend]] is steadfast also in truth. He is kind and withholds that which is not good, but he always, always speaks towards truth. He says things that might make people dislike him or might cause a ruckus in different company. He speaks in good faith to me, always. In little things and in the things that matter most, he seeks to understand deeply and discusses in clear terms the results of his findings.

In truth, when it comes to things that I care about, I get scared to speak what I think. The open dialogue becomes a place to fail. When asked directly what I think and stand in the conversation, I diminished the question, spoke vaguely around the answer, and ultimately provided a (not incorrect, but) reactive response to his question. 

But he would never test me like that, for he is a gentle friend in search of truth, not inflating his own ego. I am capable of having the discussion he wants to have. I am sorry, friend--you admitted to being a butt, but I must apologize to you for being a butt as well. I ragebaited you by putting the burden on *you* to explain yourself rather than explaining what I believe. Out of my own fears, I did this. I have a deep fear of division, and ending up in a hole that I am not capable of leaving by my own power. 

If I could go back, I would take a deep breath to remember who I am, and respond in a way that respects both my friend and my Lord. I love the things of God. I want him to talk to me about Christianity ethics and engage him in things we both find interesting. I would breathe... and then explain the simple answer before giving him the larger frame of sin, and the framework of God's plan for us. If I want to "influence people for good" like my friend does for me, I must speak with him not from my fear, but from a commitment to choose truth ([[using my agency]]).

If I cannot have this conversation or any conversation with him again, Lord, I know his soul is in your hands, and I entrust him to you. I pray for him always, too, and pray that you lead him in your paths.
